Real Home

Do you worry about your body more than you want to?
Do you catch yourself eating less just to “look better”?
Do you dress or pose a certain way because you feel watched?
Do you keep shrinking yourself to fit what others expect?

Introduction & Campaign Goals

A campaign exposing how “love” and social expectations shrink girls into smaller versions of themselves.

With dark humor and bold visuals, it helps girls and women recognize these pressures and reclaim their identity.

Audience + Context

For young women, teen girls, parents, and educators shaped by beauty culture and gender expectations, inviting them to notice how body control and “soft” restrictions show up at home and in society.

Brand Identity System

Bright, soft colors and geometric, cartoon-like forms balance dark humor with serious themes.

​

Doll Home: pearl chains, ornate shells, ribbon-tied hands, beautiful things that confine.

Real Home: diverse girls of every body and identity, free to define themselves.

Brand Identity System

Three formats—Zine, Poster Series, Website—built around one idea: labels can be removed, identities reclaimed.

Zine

A girl raised in the restrictive “Doll Home,” where love becomes an excuse to bind, shrink, and silence her.

As the shell cracks open, she learns her hunger, body, and choices were never flaws.

The story ends in an inclusive Real Home, with a peel-off label inviting readers to claim their own identity.

Poster

The oppressive “Doll Home,” covered in labels like “Be quiet” and “Stay pretty,” hides the empowering “Real Home” beneath.

Viewers peel away the labels to reveal the second layer—turning unlabeling into a physical act.

Website

A safe space to share experiences with body shame, find support and resources, and break free from harmful labels.

Reflection on Process & Campaign Relevance

The project explores trauma, body image, and gender norms through visual design, using humor and symbolism to create emotional accessibility.

Through iterative sketching, user feedback, and material experimentation, the campaign evolved into a system that is both critical and hopeful.

It is relevant today because body shame, appearance-based expectations, and “nice girl” conditioning remain deeply embedded in families, culture, and media.
